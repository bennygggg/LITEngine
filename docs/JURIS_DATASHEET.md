# Juris training corpus: datasheet

Written for the corpus `scripts/juris/` builds. Numbers are from `lit-backend/data/juris/reports/` (corpus_funnel.json, gate_breakdown_sample.json, final.json).

**What it is.** 107,847 Indian High Court criminal-appeal judgments, decided 2008-2026 across 41 benches, each labelled `succeeds` (the appeal was
allowed in whole or in part) or `dismissed` (on the merits). Split by decision year: training up to 2021 (67,748), validation 2022 (7,526), test 2023
onward (32,573).

**Why it exists.** To train and evaluate an appeal-outcome model on text that exists *before* the court's own reasoning, at a scale (about 3x the ILDC
Supreme Court corpus) and with a measured leakage ladder (full text, order removed, pre-reasoning text).

**Source.** *Indian High Court Judgments* (Dattam Labs, AWS Open Data, CC-BY-4.0), scraped from the eCourts portal: a metadata record per order with
the court's own disposal field, and the final order as a PDF. Attribution is required in anything published from it.

**Collection and labels.**
- Criminal appeals are chosen by an explicit list of case-type codes parsed from the title; look-alikes (sentence-suspension applications, leave to appeal, revisions, bail applications) are excluded.
- Label = the court's disposal field mapped to the binary target; "disposed of" records are labelled from the order text only where that text is clear.
- Removed, with counts in the funnel: unreadable PDFs (about 4% of a random sample), procedural endings such as abated, withdrawn, not pressed or delay not condoned (about 11%), records without a clear verdict (about 12%), documents under 150 words (about 7%), metadata/text contradictions (about 0.8%), near-duplicates (about 7% of the survivors), and interim bail or suspension orders and documents under 800 words (the rest).
- Where the court record and the order text are both decisive they agree in 98.2% of 9,600 sampled cases. A human audit sheet (200 cases) is generated by `scripts/juris/export_release.py` and has not been filled in yet.

**What is NOT in it.** No court text is redistributed: the release file holds identifiers (CNR), bench, year, appeal side, label, split and the S3 key of the source PDF. Party and judge names are masked in the working copy, and a label-free list of proper nouns is removed from the model's vocabulary.

**Known biases and limits.**
- High Court only; the benches are those the archive covers, and their sizes differ by an order of magnitude.
- Success rates differ widely by bench in the source records (one bench shows about 95%) and drift upward over the years (about 55% in training years, 66% in the test period).
- About one case in nine is a bail-type appeal (success about 75%, against 57% for the rest).
- The appeal side (accused or State) is read from the title's party order; a private complainant appealing an acquittal looks like an accused when the State is the respondent.
- Labels come from court records and may contain residual noise.

**Intended use.** Research on leakage-aware appeal-outcome estimation, and the indicative estimate in the LIT Engine app. **Not** for decisions that affect a person's liberty or rights, and not as legal advice.

**Maintenance.** Rebuilt with the commands in `lit-backend/scripts/juris/README.md`; the pipeline is deterministic for a fixed corpus (the frozen model reproduces its reported test numbers exactly).
