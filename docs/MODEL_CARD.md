# Outcome estimate — model card

Machine-readable source: `lit-backend/data/outcome_dataset/model/model_card.json` (generated by
`scripts/build_model_card.py`; the app reads it at runtime so the UI never states a number the model doesn't have).

## What it is

A logistic regression that estimates the probability that a **criminal appeal** gets some appellate relief
(`succeeds`, which includes partial relief) rather than being dismissed.

| | |
|---|---|
| Training data | 187 Indian criminal-appeal judgments (74 succeeded, 113 dismissed). Labels were derived from judgment text by rules, so label noise is part of every accuracy figure. |
| Cross-validated accuracy | **71.6 %** (5-fold, std 5.8 pts), macro-F1 0.688 |
| Majority-class baseline | 60.4 % |
| Legacy hand-weighted heuristic | 39.6 % (it says "succeeds" for nearly everything) |
| Scope | Criminal appeals only. Civil and other matters get facts, precedents and the argument map, and no estimate. |
| Artifact | `outcome_classifier_binary.joblib` (sha256 `62e058be…`), committed with the backend. |

## What it actually learned

| Input | Coefficient | Training range | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Appeal type: by the accused | **+1.10** | — | Dominant factor |
| Appeal type: by the State / complainant | **−0.64** | — | Dominant factor |
| Appeal type: not specified | −0.45 | — | |
| Case complexity | +0.42 | 0.50 – 0.80 | Crude (issue/statute counts) |
| Court level | −0.64 | 0.50 – 0.60 | Confounded; not a strategy lever |
| Statutory strength | +0.11 | 0.30 – 0.90 | |
| Precedent alignment | −0.04 | 0.40 – 0.95 | Mean similarity of retrieved judgments |
| Argument completeness | +0.01 | 1.00 – 1.00 | **Constant in training; carries no information** |

**Plain-language summary.** The model is, in effect, a base-rate-by-appeal-type indicator: an appeal by the accused
sits around three-in-four, an appeal by the State around one-in-three, and the other inputs move the figure by only a
few points. Moving any single non-appellant input across its realistic range shifts the probability by 0.2–3 points.
Swapping the appellant swings it by about 40 points. The product is built to say exactly that, and not more.

## How the product presents it

- Rounded to the nearest 5 % and clamped to 5–95 %; no confidence interval is claimed.
- Wording is "indicative research estimate", with the main driver named ("mostly reflects who is appealing").
- Contribution bars are **centred** (`coef × (x − training mean)`), so they show movement relative to a typical case and
  sum to `logit − baseline`.
- The legacy heuristic is shown only as a collapsed, "uncalibrated" comparison row.
- `appellant_type` and `filing_date` are never inferred (see `PROTOCOL.md`).
- Scenarios re-score the real model. They show how the *model* responds, not a prediction that changing a fact changes
  the real outcome, and they state "negligible effect" when that is the answer.

## Known limitations

1. Small sample (187), single split family; the accuracy gain over the base rate is modest.
2. Features are counts and averages from a rule-based case profile, not legal merit. Different extraction methods
   (rules vs LLM-assisted) can move the inputs; the model was trained on rule-extracted profiles.
3. `precedent_alignment` depends on the MiniLM embedder and the legacy 500-word chunking. **Do not swap the embedder or
   re-chunk the library without re-validating this model.**
4. The training and demo libraries may overlap with real judgments; the demo matter and sample matters are fictional.
5. Not legal advice. Not validated for any use that affects a person's liberty or rights.

## Changing it

Retraining is out of scope for v1.0.0. A new model must be trained and gated by `scripts/outcome_dataset/`, its
`model_card.json` regenerated with `scripts/build_model_card.py`, the scenario tests re-run (they assert the
appellant swing and the near-zero effect of the other levers), and the demo re-recorded (see `DEMO.md`).
